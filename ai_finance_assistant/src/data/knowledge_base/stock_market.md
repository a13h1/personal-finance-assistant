# Stock Market

The stock market is a collection of markets and exchanges where the buying, selling, and issuance of shares in publicly held companies take place. It serves as a critical mechanism for companies to raise capital and for investors to participate in economic growth.

## How the Stock Market Works

When a company wants to raise money, it can offer ownership shares to the public through an Initial Public Offering (IPO). Once listed on an exchange, these shares trade freely among investors. The price of a stock is determined by supply and demand—what buyers are willing to pay and what sellers are willing to accept.

Major U.S. stock exchanges include:
- **NYSE (New York Stock Exchange):** The world's largest exchange by market capitalization, home to established blue-chip companies
- **NASDAQ:** Known for technology and growth companies; operates electronically
- **CBOE:** Primarily an options and derivatives exchange

## Market Indices

Stock market performance is tracked through indices, which measure the combined performance of a group of stocks:

- **S&P 500:** Tracks 500 large U.S. companies and is widely considered the best measure of the overall U.S. stock market
- **Dow Jones Industrial Average (DJIA):** Tracks 30 prominent U.S. companies; the oldest and most widely recognized index
- **NASDAQ Composite:** Tracks all companies listed on NASDAQ, heavily weighted toward technology

## Bull and Bear Markets

A **bull market** is a period of rising stock prices, typically defined as a 20% gain from recent lows. Bull markets are associated with economic growth, low unemployment, and investor confidence.

A **bear market** is a period of declining stock prices, typically defined as a 20% drop from recent highs. Bear markets are often associated with economic recessions and reduced corporate earnings.

## Stock Valuation

Investors use various metrics to assess whether a stock is fairly valued:

**Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio:** Compares a company's stock price to its earnings per share. A higher P/E suggests investors expect higher growth.

**Price-to-Book (P/B) Ratio:** Compares market price to book value (assets minus liabilities).

**Dividend Yield:** Annual dividends divided by stock price; indicates income generated relative to price.

**Market Capitalization:** Total value of all shares outstanding. Large-cap stocks (over $10 billion) are generally more stable; small-cap stocks offer more growth potential but higher risk.

## Market Hours and Trading

U.S. stock markets are open Monday through Friday, 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM Eastern Time. Pre-market and after-hours trading also occur but with lower liquidity and wider spreads.

## Long-Term Historical Performance

Despite periods of dramatic decline, the U.S. stock market has historically returned approximately 7-10% annually over long periods. Staying invested through downturns has been rewarded consistently over multi-decade horizons.
