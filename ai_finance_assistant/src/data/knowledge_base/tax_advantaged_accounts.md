# Tax-Advantaged Accounts

Beyond the 401(k) and IRA, the U.S. tax code provides several additional tax-advantaged accounts that can significantly reduce your lifetime tax burden when used strategically.

## Health Savings Account (HSA)

The HSA is often called the "triple tax advantage" account and is widely considered the most powerful tax-advantaged account available.

### HSA Triple Tax Advantage
1. **Contributions are tax-deductible** (or pre-tax if through payroll)
2. **Growth is tax-free** inside the account
3. **Withdrawals are tax-free** when used for qualified medical expenses

### HSA Requirements and Limits (2024)
- Must be enrolled in a High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP)
- Contribution limits: $4,150 for individuals, $8,300 for families
- Additional $1,000 catch-up contribution if age 55+
- Funds roll over year to year (unlike FSAs)
- After age 65, can withdraw for any purpose (taxed as ordinary income, like a Traditional IRA)

### HSA Investment Strategy
The optimal HSA strategy is to:
1. Contribute the maximum each year
2. Invest the funds in low-cost index funds (don't leave it in cash)
3. Pay current medical expenses out of pocket
4. Save receipts indefinitely—there is no time limit to reimburse yourself
5. Let the account grow for decades, then reimburse accumulated expenses tax-free

## 529 College Savings Plans

A 529 plan is a tax-advantaged savings account specifically designed to pay for education expenses.

### Key 529 Features
- **Contributions:** Not federally deductible, but many states offer a state tax deduction
- **Growth:** Tax-free inside the account
- **Withdrawals:** Tax-free for qualified education expenses (tuition, books, room and board)
- **Contribution limits:** Up to $18,000/year per beneficiary without gift tax implications (2024)
- **No income limits:** Anyone can contribute regardless of income
- **Superfunding:** Can contribute 5 years of gifts at once ($90,000 per beneficiary)
- **SECURE 2.0 (2024+):** Up to $35,000 can be rolled into a Roth IRA if the plan is 15+ years old

### 529 Investment Options
Plans offer age-based options that automatically shift from stocks to bonds as the beneficiary approaches college age, or you can select your own investment mix.

## Flexible Spending Account (FSA)

An FSA allows you to set aside pre-tax dollars for medical or dependent care expenses.

- **Healthcare FSA:** Up to $3,200 (2024); use it or lose it by year-end (with a small grace period/carryover)
- **Dependent Care FSA:** Up to $5,000 for child or elder care; reduces taxable income

## Defined Benefit Pension Plans

Traditional pensions (more common in government and union jobs) guarantee a monthly payment in retirement based on years of service and salary history. While increasingly rare in the private sector, they provide guaranteed income and shift investment risk to the employer.

## Self-Employed Retirement Accounts

Self-employed individuals have access to powerful retirement accounts:

**SEP-IRA (Simplified Employee Pension):**
- Contribute up to 25% of net self-employment income
- Maximum $69,000 (2024)
- Simple to administer; no annual filing required

**Solo 401(k):**
- For self-employed with no employees (except a spouse)
- Contribute as both employee ($23,000) and employer (25% of compensation)
- Maximum $69,000 total (2024)
- Can make Roth contributions
- Best for high-earning self-employed individuals

## Maximizing Tax-Advantaged Space

The order of priority:
1. 401(k) to employer match
2. HSA maximum (if eligible)
3. Roth IRA maximum
4. 401(k) maximum
5. 529 if saving for children's education
6. Taxable brokerage account
