# Dollar Cost Averaging

Dollar Cost Averaging (DCA) is an investment strategy where you invest a fixed dollar amount at regular intervals—weekly, monthly, or quarterly—regardless of market conditions. Instead of trying to time the market, DCA ensures consistent investing through ups and downs.

## How Dollar Cost Averaging Works

Suppose you invest $500 every month in a stock or ETF:

| Month | Price | Shares Purchased | Total Shares | Total Invested |
|-------|-------|------------------|--------------|----------------|
| Jan   | $50   | 10.0             | 10.0         | $500           |
| Feb   | $40   | 12.5             | 22.5         | $1,000         |
| Mar   | $45   | 11.1             | 33.6         | $1,500         |
| Apr   | $55   | 9.1              | 42.7         | $2,000         |

Average purchase price: $47.25 per share
Simple average of prices: $47.50 per share

Because you buy more shares when prices are low, DCA naturally results in an average cost lower than the simple average price over the period.

## DCA vs. Lump-Sum Investing

Research (including a Vanguard study) shows that lump-sum investing outperforms DCA approximately 2/3 of the time over 12-month periods—because markets tend to rise over time, and getting money invested sooner means more time in the market.

However, DCA has significant advantages:

**When DCA wins:**
- Market declines after initial investment
- Reduces regret risk (investing everything just before a crash)
- Better for investors without a lump sum available

**When lump-sum wins:**
- Markets are rising consistently
- Investor has cash available and long time horizon

For most working people who invest from their paycheck, DCA is the natural approach—and an excellent one.

## Psychological Benefits

The greatest advantage of DCA may be behavioral:

1. **Removes emotion:** Investing automatically removes the temptation to time the market
2. **Reduces decision fatigue:** No need to decide when or how much to invest each time
3. **Enforces discipline:** Regular automatic investments build good habits
4. **Reduces regret:** Spreading investments reduces the pain of a poorly-timed large purchase

Warren Buffett observed that "The stock market is a device for transferring money from the impatient to the patient." DCA helps investors stay patient.

## Setting Up Automatic DCA

Most brokerages and 401(k) plans support automatic investing:

1. **401(k):** Payroll deductions are DCA by nature
2. **Brokerage accounts:** Set up automatic monthly purchases of your chosen ETFs
3. **Robo-advisors:** Services like Betterment and Wealthfront automate DCA and rebalancing

## DCA in Different Market Environments

**Rising market:** DCA buys fewer shares over time as prices increase, slightly underperforming lump-sum
**Falling market:** DCA buys more shares at lower prices, resulting in a lower average cost
**Volatile market:** DCA smooths out the impact of volatility, providing a stable average cost

## Common DCA Mistakes

1. **Stopping during downturns:** This is the worst time to stop—you're missing the chance to buy at lower prices
2. **Inconsistent amounts:** The strategy works best with fixed amounts on a fixed schedule
3. **Investing in the wrong assets:** DCA doesn't fix a poor investment choice
4. **Holding too much cash while DCA-ing:** If you have a large lump sum, research suggests investing most of it immediately, then DCA the remainder

## DCA and Long-Term Wealth Building

For a 35-year-old investing $500/month until age 65 at 7% average returns:
- Total invested: $180,000
- Final portfolio value: ~$567,000

Start 10 years earlier at 25 with the same monthly amount:
- Total invested: $240,000
- Final portfolio value: ~$1,175,000

The additional 10 years of DCA more than doubles the final outcome.
