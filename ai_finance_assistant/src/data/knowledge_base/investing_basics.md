# Investing Basics

Investing is the process of allocating money with the expectation of generating income or profit over time. Unlike saving, which simply preserves money in a bank account, investing puts your money to work by purchasing assets that have the potential to grow in value.

## Why Invest?

The primary reason to invest is to build wealth over time. Inflation—the gradual rise in prices—erodes the purchasing power of money sitting idle. If inflation runs at 3% annually, $1,000 today will only buy about $740 worth of goods in 10 years. Investing in assets that outpace inflation helps preserve and grow your real wealth.

## The Time Value of Money

A core principle of investing is that money available today is worth more than the same amount in the future. This is because money today can be invested and earn returns. This concept drives the power of compound interest: when your returns generate their own returns, wealth grows exponentially rather than linearly.

For example, $10,000 invested at a 7% annual return becomes approximately $19,672 after 10 years, $38,697 after 20 years, and $76,123 after 30 years—without adding a single additional dollar.

## Types of Investments

**Stocks (Equities):** Ownership shares in a company. Stockholders benefit when the company grows and profits. Stocks offer the highest long-term returns historically but come with significant short-term volatility.

**Bonds (Fixed Income):** Loans made to governments or corporations that pay regular interest. Bonds are generally less volatile than stocks but offer lower returns.

**Real Estate:** Physical property that can generate rental income and appreciate in value. Real estate investing requires more capital and active management than stocks or bonds.

**Mutual Funds and ETFs:** Pooled investment vehicles that hold many securities, providing instant diversification. These are ideal for most individual investors.

**Cash and Cash Equivalents:** Savings accounts, money market funds, and CDs. These preserve capital but typically don't keep pace with inflation over long periods.

## Risk vs. Return

All investments involve some level of risk—the possibility that you will lose some or all of your money. Generally, higher potential returns come with higher risk. Understanding your personal risk tolerance is essential before investing.

## Starting to Invest

You don't need a lot of money to start investing. Many brokerage accounts have no minimums, and fractional shares allow you to buy a portion of expensive stocks. The most important step is simply to begin.

Key principles for beginning investors:
1. Start as early as possible to maximize compounding time
2. Invest consistently, regardless of market conditions
3. Diversify across asset classes and sectors
4. Keep costs low by choosing low-fee index funds
5. Avoid emotional decisions based on short-term market movements
