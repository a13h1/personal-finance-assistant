# Compound Interest

Compound interest is often called the "eighth wonder of the world," a phrase attributed to Albert Einstein. It is the process by which interest earns interest, causing wealth to grow exponentially over time. Understanding compound interest is arguably the most important concept in personal finance.

## Simple vs. Compound Interest

**Simple interest** is calculated only on the principal (original) amount.
- $10,000 at 7% simple interest for 10 years = $17,000

**Compound interest** is calculated on the principal plus all accumulated interest.
- $10,000 at 7% compound interest for 10 years = $19,672

The difference grows dramatically over longer periods:
- 20 years: Simple = $24,000 vs. Compound = $38,697
- 30 years: Simple = $31,000 vs. Compound = $76,123
- 40 years: Simple = $38,000 vs. Compound = $149,745

## The Compounding Formula

The formula for compound interest is:

**A = P × (1 + r/n)^(nt)**

Where:
- A = Final amount
- P = Principal (initial investment)
- r = Annual interest rate (as a decimal)
- n = Number of times interest compounds per year
- t = Time in years

## Compounding Frequency

The more frequently interest compounds, the more you earn:
- Annual compounding: $10,000 → $19,672 after 10 years at 7%
- Monthly compounding: $10,000 → $20,097 after 10 years at 7%
- Daily compounding: $10,000 → $20,136 after 10 years at 7%

While the difference between monthly and daily compounding is small, the difference between annual and monthly is meaningful over long periods.

## The Rule of 72

A simple mental math shortcut: divide 72 by the annual interest rate to estimate how many years it takes to double your money.

- At 6% return: 72 ÷ 6 = 12 years to double
- At 8% return: 72 ÷ 8 = 9 years to double
- At 10% return: 72 ÷ 10 = 7.2 years to double

## The Power of Starting Early

Time is the most important variable in compound interest. Consider two investors:

**Early investor:** Invests $5,000/year from age 25-35 (10 years, $50,000 total), then stops
**Late investor:** Invests $5,000/year from age 35-65 (30 years, $150,000 total)

Assuming 7% returns, at age 65:
- Early investor: ~$602,000
- Late investor: ~$472,000

The early investor contributes $100,000 less but ends up with $130,000 more—solely because of the additional 10 years of compounding.

## Compound Interest Works Against You Too

The same force that builds wealth can destroy it through debt. Credit card debt at 20% annual interest compounds against you just as powerfully.

$5,000 in credit card debt at 20% with no payments:
- After 5 years: $12,442
- After 10 years: $30,958

Always pay off high-interest debt before investing—guaranteed 20% returns through debt payoff beat almost any investment.

## Maximizing Compound Growth

1. **Start as early as possible:** Every year of delay costs exponentially
2. **Reinvest dividends:** Dividend reinvestment (DRIP) compounds returns automatically
3. **Minimize fees:** Even 1% in annual fees dramatically reduces long-term wealth
4. **Avoid withdrawals:** Taking money out resets the compounding clock
5. **Increase contributions over time:** Even small increases significantly impact final outcomes
