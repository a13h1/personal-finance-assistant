# Dividends

Dividends are payments made by a corporation to its shareholders, distributing a portion of the company's profits. They represent one of two main ways investors make money from stocks (the other being price appreciation). Understanding dividends helps investors build income-generating portfolios and identify financially healthy companies.

## How Dividends Work

When a company earns profit (net income), management can choose to reinvest it into the business, buy back shares, or distribute it to shareholders as dividends. Companies that consistently pay and grow dividends are typically mature, profitable businesses with stable cash flows.

**Dividend payment process:**
1. **Declaration Date:** Board of directors announces the dividend
2. **Ex-Dividend Date:** Must own shares before this date to receive the dividend
3. **Record Date:** Company identifies shareholders on this date
4. **Payment Date:** Dividend is distributed to shareholders

## Key Dividend Metrics

**Dividend Yield:** Annual dividend per share divided by current stock price, expressed as a percentage.
- If a stock pays $2/year and trades at $50, dividend yield = 4%
- Average S&P 500 dividend yield historically: 1.5-2%

**Payout Ratio:** Percentage of earnings paid as dividends.
- 30-50% payout ratio: Sustainable; room for growth
- 80%+ payout ratio: Potentially unsustainable; may indicate dividend risk
- 100%+ payout ratio: Company paying more than it earns; dividend likely to be cut

**Dividend Growth Rate:** How fast the dividend payment increases over time. Companies that consistently grow dividends (Dividend Aristocrats have raised dividends for 25+ consecutive years) often outperform.

## Types of Dividends

**Cash Dividends:** Most common; paid in cash directly to your account.

**Stock Dividends:** Additional shares paid instead of cash; dilutes existing shares but no immediate tax impact.

**Special Dividends:** One-time payments, often from asset sales or exceptional profits. Not expected to recur.

**Return of Capital:** A portion of your original investment returned, rather than profits distributed. Reduces your cost basis.

## Dividend Investing Strategies

**Dividend Growth Investing:** Focus on companies that consistently increase their dividends. Companies like Johnson & Johnson, Procter & Gamble, and Coca-Cola have raised dividends for 50+ consecutive years. The growing income stream combats inflation.

**High-Yield Dividend Investing:** Focus on stocks with above-average yields. Can provide strong current income but requires careful analysis—high yields sometimes signal financial distress.

**Dividend ETFs:** Funds focused on dividend payers. Examples:
- VYM (Vanguard High Dividend Yield ETF): ~3% yield, diversified
- SCHD (Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF): High quality, growing dividends
- DVY (iShares Select Dividend ETF): Higher yield focus

## Dividend Reinvestment (DRIP)

Most brokerages offer Dividend Reinvestment Plans (DRIPs) that automatically use dividends to purchase additional shares. This compounds your investment by continuously adding to your position.

**Example of DRIP compounding:**
$10,000 invested in a stock with 3% dividend yield and 5% price appreciation:
- Without DRIP (10 years): Portfolio grows to ~$16,289
- With DRIP (10 years): Portfolio grows to ~$18,061

## Tax Treatment of Dividends

**Qualified dividends:** Taxed at lower capital gains rates (0%, 15%, or 20%). Most dividends from U.S. stocks held for the required period qualify.

**Ordinary dividends:** Taxed as regular income. Includes dividends from REITs, money market funds, and some foreign companies.

Hold dividend-paying stocks in tax-advantaged accounts (IRA, 401k) when possible to defer or eliminate dividend taxes.

## Dividend Safety Assessment

Before relying on dividends for income, assess:
1. **Payout ratio:** Below 60% is generally safer
2. **Earnings trend:** Are earnings growing or declining?
3. **Debt levels:** High debt can crowd out dividend payments
4. **Dividend history:** Has the company cut dividends before?
5. **Free cash flow:** Dividends should be covered by cash generation, not just accounting earnings
